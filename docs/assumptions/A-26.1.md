# A-26.1

**Assumption.** In Code Crossing, each gap in a lane's train rolls on its own for an orb: a 15% chance (`ORB_CHANCE`), at a random cell inside the gap. Taking one removes only that orb, and orbs come back on the next level. Each level shuffles the speed ranks across the lanes and scales every lane by a random 0.85 to 1.15. No lane runs faster than the top rank does at that level without jitter, and the reduced motion cap still holds.

**Reason.** Before this (#26), a lane had one orb slot repeated in every gap and one taken flag, so orbs came a full row at a time and vanished together. A per-gap hash of the lane's seed scatters orbs across the board and keeps arriving as the trains scroll, with no list of orb objects to grow. Capping the jitter at the level's top speed keeps every level no harder to cross than before, which the fairness autopilot checks.

**Effect if incorrect.** At 15% there are about 2 to 5 orbs on a desktop board at once and 0 to 2 on a phone. If that is too few or too many, `ORB_CHANCE` is a single number to tune. If levels should feel faster, the jitter could be allowed past the top rank, but the autopilot would have to be rechecked at 375px wide, where it lost a life on level 3 with a ±15% jitter and no cap.

**Validation or mitigation.** World tests cover per-gap pickup, the orb rate, orbs never sitting on a block, and the speed bounds. The fairness autopilot crosses three times with no lost life at five sizes, eight seeds each, with and without reduced motion. Play-test on a phone to judge how many orbs feel right.
